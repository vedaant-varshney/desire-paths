# Desire Paths
*Desire Paths* is a visual showcase of the human nature to take the path of least resistance and the value in breaking from authority. In an environment devoid of color, travellers traverse the designated path, no matter how inefficient in may be. Over time, travellers diverge from the known path, imprinting *desire paths* into the environment, often shorter and quicker than the original road.

- Ensure full screen for smooth transitions

### Formal Qualities

*Desire Paths* brings viewers into an overhead view of an abstract city or town with three initial features. One is the main path upon which travellers may traverse across without being impeded, the second are "buildings" which are impassable structures, and the last is a semi-permeable fence which may be crossed. The main path leads from a start point to an ending point, but is intentionally an inefficient route. Initially, walkers spawn into the scene, with a high likelihood of following the conventional main road between endpoints. As time passes, agents are more willing to experiment, leaving behind a colorful red desire path in their wake. This more efficient path deepens into the environment as greater proportions of agents take it, diverging from the initial grayscale scene.

When agents stop spawning and have all reached their destination, a new procedurally generated scene is created, and there is a smooth transition that flows from the previous end point to the new start point, giving the semblance of an endless journey. 

## Context
A desire path is an “unplanned small trail formed by erosion caused by human or animal traffic. The path usually represents the shortest or the most easily navigated route between an origin and destination” (“Desire Path”). Walking through the University of Waterloo, I have witnessed many desire paths, like the temporary shortcut through Lester Street from the Rez-One parking lot, and seeing trodden paths across several street corners near the university. Overall, these examples serve as the inspiration for *Desire Paths*. 

This work symbolizes how humans change the environment to meet their needs. In the context of this piece, it has neither a positive nor negative connotation. Desire paths created across the university serve to save students time, but can also cause damage to the environment (natural or human-made). As such, they can be thought of as a uniquely human phenomenon, born out of our nature to seek the path of least resistance. 

Additionally, creating desire paths can represent the act of breaking from existing authorities (being the ones who laid the initial road). The path set by the authorities can be needlessly complex, often not meeting the needs of the broader community. To resolve this, one can break from the path and optimize it, bringing about unofficial change. Those that follow from the optimized path also benefit from the shorter journey, while solidifying and formalizing it into place. In other words, it showcases how the collective has the power to improve and alter society, even if it must diverge from the previously set rules. Given sufficient support, the aforementioned alterations can be made official, leading to broad improvements. 

The initial grayscale color scheme and grid-like environment is meant to illicit a sense of rigidity and structure in the viewer that represents existing authorities. Desire paths take on a saturated red hue, symbolizing desire and a divergence from the norm. The splattered paths are not uniform and can follow diagonals, supplementing the idea of human nonconformity and taking the route of lower resistance.
